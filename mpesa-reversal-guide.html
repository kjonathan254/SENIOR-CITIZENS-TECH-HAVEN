How to Reverse an M-Pesa Transaction

A step-by-step guide for older adults in Kenya

✍️ Kevin Jonathan Otieno 📅 July 2026 ⏱️ ~6 min read 💰 Finance & M-Pesa

Quick Summary

If you sent money to the wrong number, do not panic. You have two ways to reverse it: dial *456# and follow the prompts, or forward the original M-Pesa SMS to 456. Do not send another payment. Do not share your PIN with anyone — not even someone claiming to be from Safaricom.

First, Breathe. This Happens Every Day.

If you are reading this because you just realised, "Nimetuma pesa kwa namba sio yake" — you are not alone. Hundreds of Kenyans send money to the wrong number every single day. Safaricom knows this. They built a system specifically to help you get your money back.

You are not foolish. You are not "too old for technology." You made a small mistake, and there is a clear path to fix it.

Take a deep breath. We will walk through this together, step by step.

Method 1: The Fastest Way (Forward the SMS)

This is the simplest method. You do not need to remember any codes.

  1. Find the original M-Pesa SMS that confirmed your payment. It looks like this:
    "SLK4H7R2T1 Confirmed. KSh 2,000 sent to JOHN DOE 0712 345 678 on 28/7/26..."
  2. Forward that exact SMS to 456.
    (Open the message → Tap and hold → Select "Forward" → Type 456 → Send.)
  3. Wait. Safaricom will reply with instructions or confirmation.
Important: Do not delete this original SMS. It contains your Transaction ID — the code Safaricom needs to find your payment.

Method 2: Using the USSD Menu

If you cannot find the SMS, use this method.

  1. On your phone, dial *456# and press Call.
  2. Select "Cancel / Reverse Transaction" (or "Reversal").
  3. Enter your Transaction ID (e.g., SLK4H7R2T1) from the SMS.
    Don't have it? Select the recent transaction from the list if shown.
  4. Confirm the details on your screen.
  5. Enter your M-Pesa PIN.
  6. Press OK.

You will receive an SMS confirming whether the reversal was successful.

What the SMS Reply Will Say

If the reversal worksIf the reversal fails
"Reversal Request [Transaction ID] has been successfully processed. KSh [Amount] has been credited back to your M-Pesa account." "Reversal Request [Transaction ID] failed. The recipient has insufficient funds or has already withdrawn the money. Please contact Safaricom Customer Care on 100."

If it fails, do not give up. Read the section below: "What If It Doesn't Work?"

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

MistakeWhy It Hurts You
Hanging up mid-menuUSSD menus time out quickly. If you stop reading, the system closes and you start over.
Dialing *234# or *100# insteadThese are general Safaricom menus. The reversal shortcode is *456#.
Deleting the original SMSWithout the Transaction ID, reversal becomes much harder.
Sending a second paymentNever try to "fix" a wrong transfer by sending more money. This only makes things worse.
Sharing your PINSafaricom will NEVER ask for your PIN to reverse money. Anyone who asks is a scammer.

🚨 Watch Out for Scammers

After a wrong transfer, you may receive a call from someone pretending to help. Here are three fake messages or calls to ignore:

  1. "I am from Safaricom. Please tell me your PIN so I can reverse the money immediately."
    → This is a lie. Safaricom never asks for your PIN.
  2. "Send me KSh 50 'processing fee' and I will reverse your transaction."
    → This is a lie. Reversals are free. No fees are required.
  3. "I am the person who received your money. Send me your M-Pesa PIN so I can send it back."
    → This is a lie. No honest person needs your PIN to return money.

Rule: If anyone asks for your PIN, hang up. Then call Safaricom directly on 100 or 234.

What If It Doesn't Work?

Sometimes the reversal fails because the recipient has already withdrawn the money. Here is what to do next:

  1. Call Safaricom Customer Care: 100 or 234.
    Explain calmly: "Nimetuma pesa kwa namba sio yake. Reversal imeshindikana." (I sent money to the wrong number. The reversal failed.)
  2. Safaricom will investigate. If the money is still in the recipient's M-Pesa account, they will freeze it and contact the person for consent to return it.
  3. If the recipient refuses to return the money, Safaricom will guide you on filing a police report. This is rare, but it is an option.
Time matters. Reversals submitted within 24 hours have the highest chance of success. After 24 hours, you can still request help, but it requires manual investigation.

Remember

  • You are not alone. This happens every day.
  • Your money is not automatically lost. Safaricom has systems to help.
  • Your PIN is yours alone. Never share it.
  • When in doubt, call 100. A real Safaricom agent will never rush you or ask for your PIN.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I reverse an M-Pesa transaction sent to the wrong number?

Forward the original M-Pesa confirmation SMS to 456, or dial *456# and select Reversal, then enter your Transaction ID and M-Pesa PIN. Safaricom will reply by SMS confirming whether the reversal succeeded.

What is the M-Pesa reversal code?

The M-Pesa reversal shortcode is *456#. Some people mistakenly dial *234# or *100#, which are general Safaricom menus and will not process a reversal.

Why did my M-Pesa reversal fail?

Usually because the recipient has already withdrawn the money or has insufficient funds to cover it. Call Safaricom Customer Care on 100 or 234 — they can investigate and, if funds remain, freeze them and request the recipient's consent to return the money.

Will Safaricom ask for my M-Pesa PIN to reverse a transaction?

No. Safaricom will never ask you to share your PIN by phone call or message. You only ever enter your PIN yourself, directly into the *456# menu. Anyone who asks for it is a scammer.

How long do I have to reverse an M-Pesa transaction?

Reversals submitted within 24 hours have the highest chance of success. You can still request help afterward, but it requires manual investigation and may take longer.

Does M-Pesa charge a fee to reverse a wrong transaction?

No. Reversals are free. Anyone asking for a "processing fee" is running a scam.
